01 · WHY THIS PLATFORM EXISTS

We cook for the one customer who can't complain

A baby cannot tell you a jar tasted wrong, arrived warm, or came from a tired batch. Their parents can only trust — and trust is not a feeling we ask for, it is a system we can show. Every module in the Tiny Tummy platform exists to make one promise checkable: we can prove where every meal came from, how it was made, who made it, and when it reached your door.

The platform is one operations dashboard covering the entire business: orders, kitchen production, inventory, purchasing, delivery, quality, customer care, and people. Nothing lives on paper alone, and nothing important lives in one person's memory.

02 · FOOD SAFETY BY DESIGN — ISO 22000

The standard is built into the screens, not filed in a drawer

ISO 22000 is the international framework for food safety management. Most small kitchens treat it as a binder they open before an audit. We inverted that: the requirements live inside the dashboard as a working checklist — clause by clause (context, leadership, planning, support, operation, evaluation, improvement), with the PRPs (cleaning, pest control, supplier approval, hygiene, water) and the full HACCP chain (flow diagrams, hazard analysis, CCPs with critical limits, monitoring records).

  • Every requirement carries a flag — done in progress not done — plus a named owner and an evidence reference.
  • A readiness bar shows the whole company one honest number, reviewed monthly by management.
  • Several requirements are not documents at all — they are the software itself: FEFO rotation, lot traceability and complaint-to-lot linking are ticked off by the way the system works.
  • Twice a year we run a traceability drill: pick a delivered order, trace back to every ingredient lot in under 30 minutes.
For a kitchen feeding babies, food safety is not a department. It is the operating system. The checklist is our working summary; certification work follows the official standard text.
03 · WHY LOT = STOCK

Nothing enters our kitchen anonymously

In our inventory there is no such thing as "12 kg of carrots." There is lot CAR-240825 — 8 kg, expiring on a known date, sitting on a named shelf — and lot CAR-240818 next to it. Stock is not a number; stock is a set of lots. That single design decision powers everything below:

⏳

FEFO, enforced

Picking always takes the First-Expired, First-Out lot. The screen shows which lot is next; fresher stock physically cannot jump the queue unnoticed.

🧾

Invoice = receiving

Posting a supplier invoice IS the stock entry: each line (ingredient, quantity, cost, lot number, expiry) becomes a lot instantly. No separate receiving step to forget, no unlabeled delivery accepted at the door.

🚨

Recall in minutes

If a supplier lot is ever suspect, we know exactly which production batches used it and exactly which customers received those batches — before the end of the phone call.

💰

Honest stock value

Every lot carries its own purchase cost, so stock value, food cost and margins are computed from reality, not averages.

…and why every product carries a lot number too

When an order is packed, the system prints a batch lot label (e.g. B-2026-040-0828) onto it. That label is the knot that ties both directions of the chain together:

One label, two directions: any complaint finds its ingredients; any ingredient finds its customers.
04 · QUALITY CONTROL AT THE LINE

Every meal passes gates a person must sign

Production is not a checklist of good intentions — it is a sequence of signed, timed, measured steps. Two of them are HACCP Critical Control Points with hard limits the software refuses to bend:

GateTestLimitIf it fails
🔥 CookingCore temperature measured and recorded≥ 85 °CThe step cannot close. Re-cook, measure again; the failure is logged and the manager alerted.
❄️ Blast chillTemperature after chilling, within 90 min≤ 5 °CSame — the step stays open until the food is safely cold, and the excursion is on record.
⚖️ PortioningActual portions and average weight vs the product's target± 5 %Recorded anyway; outside tolerance alerts the manager — short portions and giveaways both show up in margin.
✅ Final QCLabel vs order, seal, weight sample, cold chainPass requiredNothing reaches dispatch without the QC signature.

Each step also carries a planned time from the recipe. Starting a step starts a timer; when the planned minutes are up, a buzzer sounds in the kitchen and the step turns red until it is handled. Overdue production is never silent.

05 · THE BOM — ONE RECIPE, THE WHOLE TRUTH

The recipe card is also the cost sheet and the production plan

Every product's Bill of Materials holds three kinds of lines, and together they run the factory:

Ingredient lines — exact quantity per portion, in the unit the kitchen actually uses (grams, kilograms, liters, pieces — converted automatically to the stocked unit). These lines drive FEFO stock consumption the moment an order enters the kitchen.

Process steps — every named step of production ("Carrot cooking", "Meat preparation", portioning, chilling, packing…), each with its planned minutes and its own labor rate. These minutes drive the kitchen clock, the alarms, the station workload chart — and the labor cost.

The full cost — materials plus labor, per portion and per batch, with food-cost % flagged when it crosses target. Margins everywhere in the platform use this full cost, so a product is never "profitable" only because nobody counted the hands that made it.

06 · THE CUSTOMER'S VOICE — FEEDBACK & COMPLAINTS REGISTER

Every message becomes a numbered case that cannot be lost

Complaints, questions, suggestions and praise all land in one register as typed records — CF-2026-006 — with a category, a priority up to critical, a named responsible person, a follow-up date, and a status that only moves through a real workflow: new → in progress → escalated → resolved.

  • Nothing waits unseen. The moment a team member opens the dashboard, a "records waiting for you" panel greets them with their open cases.
  • Pass the ball. A case can be handed to another person or a whole role with a note; the previous owner stays visible on the record and the handover is logged — responsibility moves, accountability never disappears.
  • A complaint is a quality instrument. A food-quality case links the order and its batch lot — so one parent's message about one jar automatically becomes a check on an entire lot. That is how a complaint register protects every other customer.
  • Category analytics show where problems cluster (delivery, billing, food quality…), reviewed weekly against lots.
07 · THE DELIVERY PROTOCOL

The customer hears from us before they need to ask

Most "where is my order?" calls are not complaints — they are uncertainty. Our protocol removes the uncertainty, which removes the calls, which removes effort from both the family and the team:

🚚 Left the kitchenpush with ETA from road distance› 🗺 On the roadlive map, agent GPS, cold-chain boxes› 🚪 Arriving soonpush minutes before the door› 📦 Deliveredtimestamped confirmation

The estimated arrival is computed honestly — road distance at real city speed, not wishful thinking — and the one rule that builds the most trust: if we are going to be late, the customer's phone knows before the customer does. A delay, whether reported by the agent or detected automatically when the promised time passes, immediately sends a new arrival time. Order before 3:00 PM, delivered next day; every stage of the pipeline is timestamped so the promise is measurable, not aspirational.

Confidence is the product of predictability. A parent who was told about a 15-minute delay trusts us more than one whose order arrived on time in silence.
08 · EVERY ACTION LEAVES A TRACE

The log file behind every transaction

Every object in the platform — an order, a case, an invoice, a requisition, a lot — carries its own history log: who did what, and exactly when. A single order's file reads like a flight recorder:

Above the per-object files sits the central Activity Log: one register of every action in the platform — per user, per module, with date and time — filterable by person, searchable, exportable, and append-only. One screen answers “who touched this, and when?” for the whole company.

The same discipline covers the sensitive actions: marking an invoice paid, writing off an expired lot (reason required), granting a kitchen station, escalating a case, a super-admin working as another user — all logged with both identities. When something goes wrong, we do not reconstruct events from memory; we read them. That is what makes errors traceable, fixes fast, and disputes short.

09 · PEOPLE, ROLES & STATIONS

The right hands on the right task — by permission, not habit

Access follows one key per capability, resolved per person → role → super admin, and enforced in the database itself, not only on the screen. In the kitchen it goes one level finer: every person holds station grants — 🔪 prep, 🔥 cooking, 🥣 assembly, ⚖️ portioning, ❄️ chilling, 📦 packaging, ✅ QC. A new helper granted only assembly and packaging simply cannot start a cooking step; the system offers each task only to the people qualified for it, and can auto-assign a whole order's steps to the least-loaded eligible hands.
